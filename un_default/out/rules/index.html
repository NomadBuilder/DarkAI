Colonial & Eurocentric

Language that centers Western defaults or frames non-Western peoples as lesser or newly found by outsiders.

  • Discovery framing

    /\bdiscovered\b/i

    “Discovered” often erases Indigenous presence and agency. Prefer language that names arrival, contact, or colonization.

    Context-sensitive options (not automatic swaps): encountered · arrived in · first documented by [who] among [people already living there]

    Rule-specific evidence · Daedalus — Climate & Language: An Entangled Crisis — documents how “discovery,” terra nullius, and colonial renaming render Indigenous presence invisible · Indigenous Journalists Association — Terminology Guide — recommends specific nation/community names and rigorous phrasing in coverage of Indigenous peoples

    discover-land

  • “New World”

    /\bnew world\b/i

    Treats the Americas as empty or newly real only upon European arrival.

    Context-sensitive options (not automatic swaps): the Americas · Turtle Island (when appropriate) · Abya Yala

    Rule-specific evidence · American Journal of Biological Anthropology — Words matter in primatology — examines Old World/New World terminology as colonial framing that can erase Indigenous history

    new-world

  • “Old World”

    /\bold world\b/i

    Context-sensitive options (not automatic swaps): Europe, Africa, and Asia · Afro-Eurasia · name the regions

    Rule-specific evidence · American Journal of Biological Anthropology — Words matter in primatology — examines Old World/New World terminology as colonial framing that can erase Indigenous history

    old-world

  • “Third World”

    /\bthird[- ]world\b/i

    Cold War hierarchy that ranks countries as behind a Western “first.”

    Context-sensitive options (not automatic swaps): name the specific countries or regions · countries subjected to colonial extraction (when that relationship is the point) · imperial core / periphery (when using that analytical framework) · low- and middle-income countries (only when income is the measure)

    Rule-specific evidence · Scotland’s International Development Alliance — Inclusive Language Guide — identifies first/third world and developing/developed language as hierarchical and tied to exploitative relations · MSF Anti-Racism Project — Equitable and Inclusive Language — recommends specificity and warns that developing world, Global South, and third world can be reductive

    Contested / context-dependent · ALNAP — Global South/North and first/third world — explains when relational power terms can be useful and when they become reductive binaries

    third-world

General inclusion

Broader phrasing that can exclude or flatten communities.

  • Hyperbolic job titles

    /\b(?:coding )?ninjas?\b|\b(?:marketing )?rockstars?\b/i

    Can feel exclusionary in hiring copy and often imports cultural metaphors casually. (UI “wizard” steps are not flagged.)

    Context-sensitive options (not automatic swaps): skilled · experienced · strong contributor

    Rule-specific evidence · Massachusetts — Recommendations for Inclusive Job Postings — recommends clear skills-focused job language instead of exclusionary jargon and archetypes

    ninja-rockstar

  • “Culture fit”

    /\bculture fit\b/i

    Often codes for hiring people like the existing team and can shut out difference.

    Context-sensitive options (not automatic swaps): values alignment · ways of working · collaboration style

    Rule-specific evidence · NIST — Workforce Diversity and Inclusion — supports structured, job-relevant criteria over subjective similarity screening

    culture-fit

  • “Master” branch

    Many projects renamed default branches to avoid master/slave echoes. Optional depending on your norms.

  • “Diversity hire”

    /\bdiversity hire\b|\bdiverse hire\b|\bdiversity candidate\b/i
