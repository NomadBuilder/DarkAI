Where the rules come from

Sources

The rule catalog is a curated heuristic set drawn from inclusive-language guidance and, separately, documented dogwhistles so people can notice coded language they may repeat without knowing. It is not an official standard — tune what matters on Rules.

On individual rules, Evidence for this rule means the linked material addresses a specific claim. It does not mean every suggested rewrite is automatically more inclusive. The original wording and each proposed alternative must be reviewed separately for audience, meaning, power, and context. Background reading provides context but is not proof, and Contestedmarks a genuine framing dispute. Links point to living documents; prefer the community’s own guidance when it differs from ours.

How the list was built

Anti-Default reviews two claims separately: whether wording deserves a contextual flag, and whether a proposed alternative is actually clearer or less harmful in that context. A source that supports the flag does not automatically validate every replacement. Category guides are background—not proof for each rule—and disputed framing is labeled contested.

Gender-inclusive

Male-default wording, binary address, and gendered job titles.

  • UN Women — Gender-inclusive language guidelines

    Direct guidance and alternatives for male-default collectives, occupational titles, and pronouns

  • GLAAD — Nonbinary People

    Nonbinary-led context for singular they and language that does not assume only two genders

LGBTQ+ respect

Outdated clinical terms, “lifestyle” framing, and affirming pronoun language.

  • Trans Journalists Association — Stylebook and Coverage Guide

    Trans-led, term-specific guidance for pronouns, identity, medical language, and anti-trans rhetoric

  • GLAAD — Terms to Avoid

    Direct guidance on homosexual, sexual preference, and lifestyle framing

  • GLAAD — Covering the Transgender Community

    Assigned-at-birth terminology and when identity details are relevant

  • interACT — Intersex Media Guide

    Intersex-led terminology and reporting guidance

Ableist language

Disability-as-insult metaphors and outdated medicalized phrasing.

  • National Center on Disability and Journalism — Style Guide

    Term-specific guidance with identity-first/person-first nuance and community preference

  • Rooted in Rights — Disability Writing Guidelines

    Disability-community-centered guidance on agency, neutral description, and harmful metaphors

  • Autistic Self Advocacy Network — Identity-First Language

    Community-led context showing why preferences vary and person-first is not a universal default

Racialized & othering

Coded hierarchy language, racialized idioms, and tech metaphors that pair light/dark or master/slave with good/bad.

  • National Association of Black Journalists — Style Guide

    Community-specific guidance on racial labels, stereotypes, and non-monolithic description

  • National Association of Hispanic Journalists — Cultural Competence Handbook

    Direct guidance on immigration status and describing legal circumstances rather than labeling people

  • Asian American Journalists Association — Style Guide

    Community-specific guidance on Asian American identities, stereotypes, names, and pronunciation

  • DC Office of Human Rights — Words Matter

    Historical context and alternatives for terms including grandfathered

Colonial & Eurocentric

Discovery myths, development hierarchies, colonial power, and casual appropriation of Indigenous terms. We prioritize Indigenous-led guidance and sources that name exploitation—not institutional prestige.

  • Indigenous Journalists Association — Reporting and Indigenous Terminology Guide

    Indigenous-led guidance on nation-specific naming, accuracy, identity, and political status

  • Wabanaki Alliance — Media & Style Guide

    Indigenous-led guidance that explicitly privileges Wabanaki perspective and expertise

  • Scotland’s International Development Alliance — Inclusive Language Guide

    Connects first/third world and development labels to hierarchy, exploitation, and colonial power

  • MSF Anti-Racism Project — Equitable and Inclusive Language

    Warns against reductive development and Global South labels; recommends naming places and material conditions

  • ALNAP — Use of language

    Treats Global North/South as contested relational terms that can expose—or flatten—colonial power

  • Inuit Circumpolar Council — Resolution on the use of Inuit

    An Inuit-led resolution calling for Inuit rather than Eskimo in research and public documents

  • Survival International — Proud, not primitive

    Connects primitive, savage, and civilized hierarchies to persecution and forced development

Age, class & workplace / tech defaults

Ageist shorthand, class-coded insults, and hiring or engineering metaphors.

  • Joseph Rowntree Foundation — Reporting Poverty

    Co-produced with people experiencing poverty; centers structural causes and agency

  • Street Sense Media — Reporter’s Guide

    Specificity and self-identification in reporting on homelessness

  • Jobs for the Future — Language Matters Guide

    Distinguishes skills, credentials, pay, and barriers instead of deficit labels such as unskilled

  • AARP — Language of Aging

    Research with adults 50+ supporting factual, non-monolithic descriptions

  • National Center to Reframe Aging — Communication Best Practices

    Evidence-based alternatives to burden, crisis, and decline narratives

  • NIST — Workforce Diversity and Inclusion

    Skills-focused hiring criteria instead of subjective similarity and culture screening

Coded language & dogwhistles

Far-right and conspiracy phrases that can look ordinary to outsiders. We flag them so people can learn — many are soft heads-ups, because intent isn’t always present. Turn the Coded category off on /rules if you only want inclusive-style checks.

  • Anti-Defamation League — Hate Symbols Database

    Term-specific documentation for 14/88, echo parentheses, Blood and Soil, Honkler, and related symbols

  • American Jewish Committee — Translate Hate

    Documents antisemitic codes while distinguishing contextual uses of terms such as globalist

  • Institute for Strategic Dialogue — Extremism Glossary

    Specialist definitions and context for extremist movements, narratives, and coded terms

  • Southern Poverty Law Center — Cultural Marxism

    Documents the phrase’s conspiracy lineage and movement into mainstream smear rhetoric
