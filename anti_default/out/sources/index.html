Where the rules come from

Sources

The rule catalog is a curated heuristic set drawn from common inclusive-language guidance. It is not an official standard, and communities do not always agree on every term — that is why you can tune rules on the Rules page.

Links point to living documents; wording on those sites may change. Prefer the community’s own guidance when it differs from ours.

How the list was built

Anti-Default’s rules were curated from common inclusive-language guidance — major style guides, journalism handbooks, disability and LGBTQ+ media guides, and tech writing norms. It is a starting set you can tune, not an official standard or original research study.

Gender-inclusive

Male-default wording, binary address, and gendered job titles.

  • APA Style — Bias-free language (gender)

    mankind, singular they, gendered roles

  • United Nations — Gender-inclusive language

    he/she pairings, chairperson, occupation titles

  • GLAAD Media Reference Guide

    binary framing and inclusive address in media

LGBTQ+ respect

Outdated clinical terms, “lifestyle” framing, and affirming pronoun language.

  • GLAAD Media Reference Guide

    homosexual, lifestyle, transgendered, sex change, biological male/female

  • APA Style — Sexual orientation

    orientation vs preference; identity-affirming wording

Ableist language

Disability-as-insult metaphors and outdated medicalized phrasing.

  • National Center on Disability and Journalism — Style Guide

    wheelchair-bound, suffers from, handicapped, mental-health metaphors

  • CDC — Communicating with and about people with disabilities

    people-first / clear disability language

Racialized & othering

Coded hierarchy language, racialized idioms, and tech metaphors that pair light/dark or master/slave with good/bad.

  • APA Style — Racial and ethnic identity

    Oriental, vague “ethnic,” identity-specific naming

  • Conscious Style Guide

    whitelist/blacklist, master/slave, grandfathered, immigration language

  • ‘Illegal immigrant’ no more — The Associated Press

    AP Stylebook shift away from labeling people as “illegal”

Colonial & Eurocentric

Discovery myths, Third World hierarchies, and casual appropriation of Indigenous terms.

  • Native American Journalists Association — resources

    Indigenous naming, accuracy, and media practice

  • Conscious Style Guide — Indigenous & colonial language

    discovery framing, appropriation metaphors (powwow, spirit animal, etc.)

  • World Bank / development language (income & region terms)

    preference for precise income/region labels over “Third World”

Age, class & workplace / tech defaults

Ageist shorthand, class-coded insults, and hiring or engineering metaphors.

  • APA Style — Age

    older adults vs “the elderly”

  • GitHub — Renaming the default branch from master

    master → main; related allowlist/denylist discussions in open source

  • Conscious Style Guide — workplace & bias

    culture fit, hyperbolic job titles, class-coded language
